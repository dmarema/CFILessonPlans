🎯 Lesson Objective (In Plain English)

By the end of this lesson, you will know when the FAA legally requires oxygen under 14 CFR § 91.211 (unpressurized and pressurized), which system & mask to use at each altitude, and how to recognize and recover from hypoxia before it incapacitates you.

The #1 Rule of 91.211: The 12,500 / 14,000 / 15,000 ft rules are based on Cabin Pressure Altitude (not just aircraft altitude). For pressurized aircraft, the FL250 / FL350 / FL410 rules are based on Aircraft Flight Altitude.

The 4 Main Types of Hypoxia

1. Hypoxic: Not enough O₂ in the lungs (altitude—most common in aviation).
2. Hypemic: Blood can't carry O₂ (CO poisoning, smoking, anemia).
3. Stagnant: Blood isn't flowing (high-G maneuvers, extreme cold).
4. Histotoxic: Cells can't use O₂ (alcohol, drugs, narcotics).

⚠️ Common PPL / CPL Student Errors & Fixes

1. Confusing Aircraft Altitude vs. Cabin Pressure Altitude

Why it happens: Students memorize "12,500 / 14,000 / 15,000" and forget it applies to cabin pressure altitude.
CFI Fix: Ask: "If you're in a pressurized King Air at 18,000 ft with a 6,000 ft cabin, do you need oxygen under 91.211(a)?" Answer: No—cabin altitude is below 12,500 ft.

2. Saying Passengers Must "Use" Oxygen Above 15,000 ft

Why it happens: Assuming crew and passenger rules are identical.
CFI Fix: Highlight the exact wording of 91.211(a)(3): Crew must be provided with and use O₂; occupants above 15,000 ft must only be provided with O₂ (though smart PICs brief them to use it!).

3. Using a Nasal Cannula Above 18,000 ft MSL

Why it happens: Cannulas are comfortable and students forget their ceiling.
CFI Fix: Above 18,000 ft MSL, ambient pressure is too low for a nasal cannula to prevent hypoxia—a mask covering nose and mouth is required.

4. Forgetting the "One Pilot Leaves the Controls" Rule (> FL350)

Why it happens: Remembering the 2-pilot quick-donning exception and forgetting what happens during a lavatory break.
CFI Fix: Above FL350, the instant one pilot leaves their seat, the remaining pilot must put on and use their mask until the other pilot returns.

5. Filling Aircraft Tanks with Medical or Welding Oxygen

Why it happens: Thinking "oxygen is oxygen."
CFI Fix: Medical O₂ contains moisture that freezes in high-altitude regulators; industrial/welding O₂ contains impurities. Only Aviator's Breathing Oxygen (ABO) is legal.
